Q1 Linguistics · Phonetics & Phonology Phonetics and Phonology · Peter Roach

What is a 'Minimal Pair' in English phonology, and what linguistic concept does it prove?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Two words that differ by only one single sound in the same position (e.g., 'ship' /ʃɪp/ and 'sheep' /ʃiːp/), proving that the two sounds are distinct phonemes that change meaning.
💡 Explication : A minimal pair (like bat/pat or ship/sheep) differs by only one sound segment in identical environment, demonstrating that /ɪ/ and /iː/ or /b/ and /p/ are phonemic contrasts capable of distinguishing lexical meaning in English.
Q2 Linguistics · Morphology English Morphology · Fromkin & Rodman

What is the key morphological difference between 'Inflectional' and 'Derivational' morphemes in English?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Derivational morphemes create new words or change grammatical category (e.g., teach -> teacher), while inflectional morphemes only alter grammatical form without changing lexical category (e.g., clean -> cleaned, cleans).
💡 Explication : English has only 8 inflectional affixes (all suffixes: -s plural, -'s possessive, -s 3rd person, -ed, -ing, -en, -er comparative, -est superlative) which never alter word class. Derivational affixes (prefixes & suffixes like re-, un-, -ful, -ize) create new words or change part of speech.
Q3 Linguistics · Syntax Transformational Generative Grammar · Noam Chomsky

In Chomskyan Generative Grammar, what is the distinction between 'Competence' and 'Performance'?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Competence is the speaker's internalized, tacit knowledge of the language system and universal grammar, while Performance is the actual observed production/use of language, subject to memory slips and fatigue.
💡 Explication : Chomsky defined 'linguistic competence' as the native speaker's idealized unconscious knowledge of syntax rules, distinct from 'linguistic performance' which reflects messy real-time utterances affected by psychological and physiological constraints.
Q4 Linguistics · Pragmatics Gricean Pragmatics · The Cooperative Principle (1975)

What are H.P. Grice's 'Four Conversational Maxims' that constitute the Cooperative Principle?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Maxim of Quantity (informative enough), Maxim of Quality (truthful), Maxim of Relation (relevant), and Maxim of Manner (clear, brief, and orderly).
💡 Explication : Grice's maxims govern cooperative conversation: Quantity (say neither more nor less than required), Quality (do not say what you believe to be false or lack evidence for), Relation (be relevant), and Manner (avoid obscurity and ambiguity).

Q8 Second Language Acquisition (SLA) Stephen Krashen's Monitor Model (1982)

According to Stephen Krashen's 'Input Hypothesis' in Second Language Acquisition, how do learners acquire language most effectively?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : When they are exposed to 'comprehensible input' (i + 1) that is slightly beyond their current level of linguistic competence in a low-anxiety environment.
💡 Explication : Krashen's Input Hypothesis posits that acquisition occurs naturally when learners understand messages containing structures just one step ahead of their current developmental stage ('i + 1'), provided their affective filter is low.

What does Larry Selinker mean by the term 'Interlanguage' in Second Language Acquisition?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : A dynamic, evolving, rule-governed linguistic system created by L2 learners that is intermediate between their native language (L1) and the target language (L2).
💡 Explication : Interlanguage is an autonomous cognitive language system built by the second language learner, possessing its own systematic grammar rules influenced by L1 transfer, overgeneralization of L2 rules, and communication strategies.

Q20 Linguistics · Historical Linguistics The Great Vowel Shift (1400-1700)

What was the 'Great Vowel Shift' (GVS) and how did it profoundly shape the Modern English language?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : A massive systematic phonological change where all Middle English long vowels were raised or diphthongized, explaining the discrepancy between modern English spelling and pronunciation.
💡 Explication : Between roughly 1400 and 1700, English long vowels shifted upwards (e.g. /iː/ became /aɪ/ in 'time', /eː/ became /iː/ in 'see'). Because the printing press fixed spelling beforehand, English retains archaic spellings.

What is 'Allophonic Variation' in English phonology?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Predictable phonetic variants of the same underlying phoneme occurring in complementary distribution (e.g., aspirated [pʰ] in 'pin' vs unaspirated [p] in 'spin') that do not change word meaning.
💡 Explication : Allophones are non-contrastive physical realizations of a single abstract phoneme. Replacing one allophone with another may sound foreign but never creates a different lexical word.
