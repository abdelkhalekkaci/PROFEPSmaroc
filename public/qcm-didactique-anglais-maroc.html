Q1 ELT Approaches & Methods Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) · Richards & Rodgers

What is the primary pedagogical goal of the Communicative Language Teaching (CLT) approach adopted in the Moroccan English curriculum?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Developing learners' communicative competence (linguistic, sociolinguistic, discourse, and strategic) in authentic real-life situations.
💡 Explication : CLT prioritizes 'communicative competence' (Hymes, Canale & Swain) over mere linguistic accuracy. The focus is on meaningful communication, authentic language use, and fluency in communicative interactions.
Q2 Lesson Structuring Frameworks PPP vs. TBL Frameworks in Moroccan Secondary Guidelines

In the standard PPP lesson model (Presentation, Practice, Production), what distinguishes the 'Production' phase from the 'Practice' phase?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Practice is controlled and focuses on accuracy, while Production is freer and encourages fluency and authentic communicative use of the target language.
💡 Explication : In PPP, the Practice stage involves controlled exercises (fill-in-the-blanks, drills) focusing on accuracy, whereas the Production stage allows students to use the newly acquired structure freely in creative tasks (role-plays, debates, writing) to foster fluency.
Q3 Teaching Receptive Skills Reading Strategies · Jeremy Harmer / Scrivener

What is the difference between 'Skimming' and 'Scanning' in reading comprehension methodology?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Skimming is reading rapidly for the main idea / gist, while scanning is reading quickly to locate specific pieces of information (names, dates, figures).
💡 Explication : Skimming involves fast reading to get the overall gist or central theme of a text. Scanning involves searching the text rapidly to spot specific isolated details or keywords without reading the entire text.
Q4 Teaching Productive Skills Process Approach to Writing in ELT

What are the sequential pedagogical stages of the 'Process Writing' approach recommended in Moroccan high schools?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Pre-writing (Brainstorming/Outlining) -> Drafting -> Revising (Content & Organization) -> Editing (Proofreading for Mechanics) -> Publishing.
💡 Explication : Process writing views writing as a recursive cognitive process rather than a one-shot product. Students generate ideas (pre-writing), draft initial thoughts, revise ideas and coherence, edit for grammatical/spelling accuracy, and share their final work.
Q5 Grammar Instruction Inductive vs. Deductive Grammar Teaching · Thornbury

What happens during an 'Inductive' approach to teaching English grammar (Guided Discovery)?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Learners analyze authentic language examples in context and discover the underlying grammatical rule for themselves with teacher guidance.
💡 Explication : An inductive approach (guided discovery) provides context and examples first, prompting students to notice patterns and formulate the rule collaboratively, leading to deeper cognitive engagement and retention compared to deductive rule-first lecturing.
Q6 Error Correction & Feedback Formative Feedback in ELT · Ur & Scrivener

During a free communicative speaking activity aiming at fluency, when and how should the teacher provide error correction?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Withhold immediate interruption to maintain fluency; take notes of common errors and provide delayed, constructive whole-class feedback afterwards.
💡 Explication : Interrupting during fluency tasks shatters confidence and communicative flow. Good practice dictates taking discreet notes of recurrent errors and conducting a delayed 'error clinic' / anonymous feedback session at the end of the activity.
Q7 Classroom Management Teacher Roles and Group Work in TEFL

What is the primary role of the EFL teacher during pair-work or small group communicative tasks?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Facilitator, monitor, and resource person who circulates unobtrusively, assists when needed, and ensures task engagement.
💡 Explication : In CLT classrooms, the teacher shifts from the 'sage on the stage' to a facilitator and monitor, managing the learning environment, guiding interactions, and gathering formative assessment data.
Q8 Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) Task-Based Learning Framework · Jane Willis

According to Jane Willis, what are the three essential stages of a Task-Based Learning (TBL) framework?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Pre-task (Introduction to topic and task) -> Task Cycle (Task, Planning, Report) -> Language Focus (Analysis and Practice).
💡 Explication : Willis's TBL framework consists of: 1) Pre-task (setting the scene and activating schema); 2) Task Cycle (carrying out the task in pairs/groups, planning report, and presenting findings); 3) Language Focus (analyzing and practicing language forms that emerged during the task).
Q9 Assessment & Evaluation in ELT Continuous Assessment & Moroccan Baccalaureate Specifications

What is the distinction between 'Formative Assessment' and 'Summative Assessment'?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Formative assessment is ongoing during learning to provide feedback and guide instruction, while summative assessment evaluates cumulative achievement at the end of an instructional unit (e.g., National Exam).
💡 Explication : Formative assessment ('assessment for learning') occurs during the learning process to diagnose gaps and adjust teaching. Summative assessment ('assessment of learning') measures final proficiency and assigns grades/certification at the end.
Q10 Remediation & TaRL in English Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) in Moroccan Pilot Schools

How does the TaRL (Teaching at the Right Level) pedagogical approach address learning gaps in Moroccan secondary English classes?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : By assessing students' actual foundational literacy and speaking levels, grouping them by learning needs rather than age/grade, and implementing targeted remediation activities.
💡 Explication : TaRL focuses on diagnosing foundational deficits (phonics, basic vocabulary, simple sentence construction) and delivering targeted, interactive remediation matched to the student's current proficiency level before tackling complex syllabus content.
Q11 Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) Checking Understanding in ELT · Workcraft

What is the pedagogical utility of Concept Checking Questions (CCQs) after presenting a new grammar structure or lexical item?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Asking targeted, simple binary/guiding questions to verify that students grasp the core meaning and usage rules without asking for abstract grammatical jargon.
💡 Explication : Asking 'Do you understand?' yields passive nods. CCQs break down the target concept into concise, unambiguous checks (e.g. for 'She used to swim': Did she swim in the past? Yes. Does she swim now? No).
Q12 Lesson Staging & Warm-up Schema Activation in Moroccan Curriculum

Why is a Warm-up / Lead-in stage essential at the beginning of an EFL secondary lesson?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : To activate learners' schemata (prior background knowledge), generate interest in the lesson topic, and ease the cognitive transition into English.
💡 Explication : A lead-in activates cognitive schemata, lowers anxiety, and prepares students conceptually and linguistically for the core reading, listening, or language input of the day.
Q13 Teaching Listening Skills Bottom-Up vs Top-Down Listening Processes

What distinguishes 'Top-Down' listening processing from 'Bottom-Up' listening processing in TEFL methodology?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Top-down relies on background knowledge and context to predict meaning, while bottom-up decodes individual acoustic cues, phonemes, words, and grammatical structures.
💡 Explication : Proficient listening coordinates both: top-down (schema-driven, utilizing world knowledge and situational clues) and bottom-up (data-driven, processing incoming acoustic phonemic signals into recognizable words).
Q14 Teaching Speaking & Fluency Information Gap Activities in CLT

Why are 'Information Gap' activities considered a cornerstone of communicative language practice?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Because a genuine communicative need is created: each learner holds information the other lacks, requiring authentic question-and-answer interaction to complete the task.
💡 Explication : Authentic communication occurs only when there is an information gap, purpose, and choice. When Student A possesses information that Student B needs to solve a problem, language becomes a real tool.
Q15 Teaching Vocabulary MFP Framework in ELT Methodology

When clarifying a new vocabulary item in EFL, what does the essential MFP framework stand for?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Meaning (definition/context), Form (part of speech, spelling, collocation), and Pronunciation (stress, phonemes).
💡 Explication : Every complete lexical presentation requires clarifying Meaning first (via pictures, mime, or context), then Form (how it is spelled and grammatical class), and finally Pronunciation (word stress and silent letters).

In the Moroccan secondary English syllabus, how are instructional units organized across approved textbooks?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Thematically and functionally (e.g., Youth Culture, Women and Power, Brain Drain, Sustainable Development), integrating language forms into relevant societal topics.
💡 Explication : Moroccan textbooks adopt a thematic-functional syllabus. Each unit addresses contemporary issues (e.g. Citizenship, Cultural Heritage, Technology) within which grammar and communication skills are naturally embedded.
Q17 Differentiated Instruction Mixed-Ability Classroom Management in Moroccan Schools

How can an English teacher effectively manage mixed-ability classes with large student numbers?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Implement tiered tasks, provide scaffolded support for struggling learners, and assign extension activities to faster finishers.
💡 Explication : Differentiation involves adapting content, process, or products: giving tiered reading tasks (guided vs open), paired peer-tutoring, and extension challenges to keep all learners appropriately engaged.
Q18 Integrating Educational Technology TICE in Moroccan Digital Education Strategy

What is the most effective pedagogical use of digital tools (ICT/TICE) in an EFL secondary classroom?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Exposing learners to authentic audiovisual materials, interactive communicative quizzes, and collaborative digital writing projects.
💡 Explication : Educational technology should enhance communicative interaction, provide authentic native-speaker listening input, facilitate formative digital checks, and promote creative collaborative output.
Q19 Pronunciation & Phonemic Awareness Word Stress and Intonation in Secondary Moroccan Classes

Why is teaching word stress patterns (e.g., 'PHO-to-graph' vs 'pho-TO-gra-phy') critical for Moroccan learners of English?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Because English is a stress-timed language where incorrect word stress significantly impairs intelligibility and native comprehension.
💡 Explication : English is stress-timed. Misplaced primary lexical stress causes greater communicative breakdown than minor phonetic mispronunciations of vowels or consonants.
Q20 Action Research & Teacher Professionalism Reflective Teaching & Moroccan CRMEF Internship

What is the definition and primary aim of 'Action Research' conducted by EFL teachers during their CRMEF practicum?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : A systematic, reflective inquiry into one's own classroom practice to identify specific learning problems, test pedagogical interventions, and improve teaching effectiveness.
💡 Explication : Action research is classroom-based reflective investigation: teachers observe a localized challenge (e.g. reticence in speaking), plan an intervention, implement it, evaluate outcomes, and refine their pedagogical stance.
Q21 ELT Approaches & Methods Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

Which feature is most characteristic of the Direct Method in English language instruction?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Exclusive use of the target language with no translation permitted and meaning conveyed through demonstration and realia.
💡 Explication : The Direct Method (Berlitz) bans L1 translation, teaching concepts directly through action, pictures, and demonstration in the target language.
Q22 Lesson Structuring Frameworks Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

In the Test-Teach-Test (TTT) staging framework, what is the role of the initial 'Test' phase?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : To diagnostically assess what learners already know about the target language point so the teacher only focuses on actual gaps during the 'Teach' stage.
💡 Explication : TTT starts with a diagnostic activity (Test 1) to reveal existing mastery and deficits, allowing tailored instruction (Teach) followed by consolidation (Test 2).
Q23 Teaching Receptive Skills Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is an effective Pre-Reading activity to activate schema in a secondary EFL classroom?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Predicting text content based on the title, pictures, headlines, and a quick brainstorming discussion.
💡 Explication : Effective pre-reading prepares the reader by predicting content, discussing visual clues, and stimulating curiosity before encountering the full text.
Q24 Teaching Productive Skills Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What distinguishes 'Cohesion' from 'Coherence' in evaluating secondary student writing?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Cohesion refers to grammatical and lexical linking devices (connectors, pronouns), while coherence refers to the logical flow, clarity, and organization of ideas.
💡 Explication : Cohesion is the grammatical glue (linking words, pronouns, transitions) connecting sentences; coherence is the macro-level logical progression and clarity of the overall thought.
Q25 Grammar Instruction Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

In teaching grammar for communication, what is the value of 'Focus on Form' compared to 'Focus on Forms'?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Focus on Form draws learners' attention to linguistic elements as they arise incidentally in communicative contexts, rather than presenting isolated grammar tables out of context.
💡 Explication : Focus on Form (Long) embeds grammatical attention within meaningful communicative activities when a breakdown or need occurs, contrasting with synthetic, isolated grammatical lecturing (Focus on Forms).
Q26 Error Correction & Feedback Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is 'Peer Feedback' (Peer Correction) and why is it beneficial in ELT writing classes?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Having learners review each other's drafts using clear rubrics or checklists, promoting learner autonomy, critical awareness, and collaborative dialogue.
💡 Explication : Peer review engages students actively in the writing process, helps them notice linguistic and structural issues from a reader's perspective, and fosters cooperative learning.
Q27 Classroom Management Teacher Talking Time (TTT) vs Student Talking Time (STT)

Why do communicative language pedagogy standards recommend reducing Teacher Talking Time (TTT) in favor of Student Talking Time (STT)?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Because language acquisition requires maximum opportunities for active student output, interaction, and productive language negotiation.
💡 Explication : Maximizing STT (70-80% of classroom time devoted to learner interaction, pair-work, and tasks) provides the communicative output necessary to develop fluency.
Q28 Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

In TBLT, what defines a 'real-world / pedagogical task' as opposed to a traditional grammar drill?

✓ Bonne réponse (A) : A task has a clear non-linguistic communicative outcome and prioritizes meaning and problem-solving over mechanical manipulation of forms.
💡 Explication : A genuine task (Nunan, Ellis) focuses primarily on meaning, involves a gap, allows learners to choose linguistic resources, and leads to a tangible communicative product (e.g. planning an itinerary, solving a puzzle).
Q29 Assessment & Evaluation in ELT Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is 'Washback' (or Backwash) in language testing theory?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : The positive or negative impact that a high-stakes exam (e.g. National Baccalaureate) exerts on classroom teaching practices and curriculum implementation.
💡 Explication : Washback describes how tests influence what and how teachers teach. Negative washback narrows instruction to test-taking tricks; positive washback aligns teaching with communicative test formats.

In the Moroccan 'Écoles Pionnières' and secondary remediation frameworks, how is diagnostic assessment used prior to remediation?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : To accurately identify specific micro-skills that students have not mastered, enabling homogeneous need-based grouping for intensive intervention.
💡 Explication : Diagnostic testing pinpoint fundamental deficits (e.g. phonics, basic decoding, sentence mechanics) so that targeted pedagogical remediation meets learners at their exact current level.
Q31 ELT Approaches & Methods Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is the core premise of the Audio-Lingual Method (ALM)?

✓ Bonne réponse (A) : Language is habit formation developed through continuous conditioning, stimulus-response, and structural pattern drills.
💡 Explication : ALM (behaviorist, Skinnerian) treated language learning as habit formation, relying heavily on choral repetition, dialogue memorization, and pattern substitution drills to avoid error fossilization.

What is the purpose of the 'Post-Reading' or 'Post-Listening' stage in a skills-based lesson?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : To provide opportunities for learners to respond to the text, connect ideas to their own lives, and produce spoken or written language integrating newly acquired insights.
💡 Explication : Post-stages serve as communicative extension: synthesizing information, debating themes, writing reactions, or applying concepts in creative projects.
Q33 Teaching Receptive Skills Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is 'Jigsaw Reading' and how does it promote cooperative communicative learning?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : An activity where different students/groups read different parts of a text, then regroup to share their information orally so everyone gains the full picture.
💡 Explication : Jigsaw reading creates an organic information gap: individual students become 'experts' on their section and must explain it clearly to peers, fostering authentic speaking and listening.
Q34 Teaching Productive Skills Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is the difference between an 'Error' and a 'Mistake' according to S.P. Corder's error analysis framework?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : An error reflects a gap in the learner's underlying linguistic competence that they cannot self-correct, while a mistake is a performance slip (due to tiredness, slip of the tongue) that the learner can self-correct when prompted.
💡 Explication : Corder demonstrated that errors reveal the learner's current interlanguage system (incompetence), whereas mistakes are temporary performance lapses of accessible knowledge.
Q35 Grammar Instruction Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is the primary role of a 'Timeline' in teaching verb tenses in an EFL secondary classroom?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : To visually clarify the relationship between past, present, and future events, aspect (continuous vs simple), and time relationships (e.g., Past Perfect before Past Simple).
💡 Explication : Timelines provide a concrete, visual schema for abstract temporal relationships, clearly demonstrating concepts like past actions preceding another past action or ongoing states.
Q36 Error Correction & Feedback Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What is 'Recasting' in oral error feedback?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Implicitly reformulating the student's incorrect utterance into the correct form without interrupting the flow of the conversation.
💡 Explication : In a recast, the teacher responds naturally with the correct form (Student: 'He go to market yesterday.' Teacher: 'Oh, he went to the market yesterday? What did he buy?'), maintaining communicative rapport.

Q38 Task-Based Language Teaching (TBLT) Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

In Jane Willis's TBL framework, what occurs during the 'Language Focus' stage at the end of the lesson?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : Students analyze and examine specific linguistic forms, collocations, and grammatical patterns that emerged naturally during the task execution.
💡 Explication : Language focus does not precede the task; it reflects on language generated during the task, consolidating forms, noticing nuances, and practicing targeted linguistic features.
Q39 Assessment & Evaluation in ELT Cadre Référentiel Officiel MENPS Maroc

What constitutes a 'Valid' language assessment instrument according to language testing standards?

✓ Bonne réponse (B) : A test that accurately measures what it purports to measure according to the specified syllabus objectives and curriculum construct.
💡 Explication : Validity (construct, content, criterion) ensures the test measures target competencies (e.g. testing communicative speaking through speaking performance, not through a written grammar test).
